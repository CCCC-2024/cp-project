Final Projects

Hi,

A big part of your grade is a final project. On Thursday Oct 6th, you will all present ONE SINGLE SLIDE about your project, so I can give you feedback. This presentation will happen in front of the class as a group. Each presentation will be strictly 1 minute long.  

How do you select a project? At the end of this message, I provide links to research pages in computational photography. These are the steps:

Step I. Select a paper (or a series of papers) that interests you from this list of research papers. You must only select a recent paper --- something published in 2023 or after. You CANNOT select a paper outside the research groups I linked below. 

Remember how we run the class. The project should be:

\- computational photography-related! Don't pick something purely machine learning, robotics or signal processing. 
\- something visually appealing to you
\- fun or interesting or that speaks to your interests or hobbies. 
\-  a paper selected such that you think you could do some part of the results in a month by end of November. 
\- NOT something that takes ton of math to understand or requires large amounts of training --- we don't have the time or the resources to support that. 

IMPORTANT: Post your group's choice our discussion page so that others know to avoid that topic. It is first come, first served. So if someone posts before you, you have to do something else. 

Step II. Given your group time restrictions, skills and interest, pick some component from this paper or papers that you selected, to work on for a project. Some ideas (you can have others):

a. You can collect your own data and run the code from the researchers' website.

b. You can use an AI to implement code from the paper PDF --- if you go this route, you should implement the entire project, not just a component. 

c. You can find a third party software or app(s) that do something similar to the paper that you selected, and compare and contrast.

d. You can extend on our class labs using ideas from the paper/project that you selected.

IMPORTANT: I will grade purely on the enthusiasm of the effort, and the interestingness of the results. I will dock points for those groups who are trying to "do the minimum" and avoid putting work in the Computational Photography class. I will definitely provide a poor grade for those who give me "AI slop" --- the presentation is to show a real project. 

thanks,
